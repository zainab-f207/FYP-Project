--- Shape #2 ---
WHAT IS SAFEVISION
--- Shape #3 ---
One project, four interlocking capabilities
--- Shape #6 ---
SafeVision is an AI-powered geospatial public-safety platform built specifically for Lahore — predicting where and when crime is likely, recommending the safer route, reading handwritten Urdu FIRs automatically, and pushing real-time alerts to nearby citizens.
--- Shape #9 ---
01
--- Shape #10 ---
Predict Crime Risk
--- Shape #11 ---
Two machine-learning models — Random Forest and Poisson — work side by side to score every area, every hour, on a 0–100 risk scale that is easy to read on the heatmap.
--- Shape #14 ---
02
--- Shape #15 ---
Recommend Safe Routes
--- Shape #16 ---
We generate three alternate paths between two points and rank them by both average risk and worst-point risk along the line. The user sees the safest, not just the fastest.
--- Shape #19 ---
03
--- Shape #20 ---
Digitise Urdu FIRs
--- Shape #21 ---
A 5-engine OCR pipeline reads Punjab Police FIRs in Urdu and English, corrects place names with a 268-entry dictionary, and verifies the law sections cited using an AI language model.
--- Shape #24 ---
04
--- Shape #25 ---
Real-time Alerts
--- Shape #26 ---
Web push notifications fire when a verified crime happens within 1.5 km of a user's home, work or current location — with a cooldown so the user is not spammed with repeated alerts.

AUTHENTICATION — LAYER 1
--- Shape #3 ---
Password hashing and signed login tokens
--- Shape #6 ---
PASSWORD HASHING
--- Shape #7 ---
bcrypt_sha256 (primary) ? bcrypt (legacy)
--- Shape #9 ---
# app/auth_updated.py:42
pwd_context = CryptContext(
  schemes=["bcrypt_sha256",
           "bcrypt"],
  deprecated="auto")
truncated = plain_password[:72]
verify(truncated, hashed_pw)
--- Shape #11 ---
WHAT THIS MEANS
--- Shape #12 ---
We never store the user's actual password — only a scrambled fingerprint of it. We use a method called bcrypt-SHA-256, which scrambles the password through TWO layers (SHA-256 first, then bcrypt). The first layer makes sure even very long passwords or names with Urdu characters keep all their security. The second layer makes the scrambled version slow to crack — even with a powerful computer.
--- Shape #15 ---
LOGIN TOKENS  ·  JWT
--- Shape #16 ---
After login the user gets a signed token — like a wristband at an event.

--- Shape #34 ---
WHAT THIS MEANS
--- Shape #35 ---
JWT (JSON Web Token) is just a string of data signed by our server. The user's browser sends it back on every request. Because only our server knows the signing key, no one can fake a valid token. We use TWO different keys — one for normal access, one for refreshes — so even if one key leaks, the attacker can't forge new sessions.

--- Shape #2 ---
AUTHENTICATION — LAYER 2 & 3
--- Shape #3 ---
Two-factor login, append-only audit log, super-admin approvals
--- Shape #6 ---
LAYER 2  ·  AUTHENTICATOR APP (OPTIONAL)
--- Shape #7 ---
Time-based 6-digit codes (TOTP)
--- Shape #8 ---
The user scans a QR code with Google Authenticator. The phone and our server then independently generate the same 6-digit number every 30 seconds. If a hacker steals your password, they still can't log in without your phone.
--- Shape #11 ---
LAYER 2  ·  EMAIL OTP (MANDATORY FOR ADMINS)
--- Shape #12 ---
Required on every admin login
--- Shape #13 ---
After typing their password, every admin and super-admin gets a 6-digit code in their email. Only after entering that code do they actually get in. There is no opt-out — admins handle FIRs, so we treat their accounts as high-value targets.
--- Shape #16 ---
AUDIT LOG  ·  APPEND-ONLY
--- Shape #17 ---
Every privileged action becomes a permanent row
--- Shape #18 ---
Whenever an admin verifies a crime, edits a user, or uploads a FIR, we write a row to the audit_logs table — capturing who did it, when, from which IP, and what changed. There is no UPDATE or DELETE path on this table by design — once written, the record stays, so any tampering is obvious.
--- Shape #21 ---
SUPER-ADMIN APPROVAL GATE
--- Shape #22 ---
6 sensitive actions need a 2nd person to approve
--- Shape #23 ---
Dangerous actions like 'delete a user' or 'promote someone to super-admin' don't happen instantly. They sit in a pending state until a second super-admin reviews and approves. This protects against a single compromised account doing damage on its own.

WHY A HYBRID, NOT A SINGLE MODEL?
--- Shape #7 ---
Random Forest

Poisson estimator
answers exactly that — 'what is the probability of a crime in the next hour?' — but is too smooth to draw clean category boundaries.
Together
they give us crisp colour categories AND a real probability — both of which the UI uses.

  max_depth        = 15,
  min_samples_leaf = 10,
  class_weight     = 'balanced',
  random_state     = 42,
  n_jobs           = -1,
)
--- Shape #11 ---
WHAT THIS MEANS
--- Shape #12 ---
Random Forest is like asking 200 different experts to look at a crime and say 'High, Medium, or Low risk' — then taking the majority vote. Each expert (decision tree) sees a slightly different slice of the data, so they don't all make the same mistakes. We tell them not to dig deeper than 15 levels (avoids overfitting) and to weight rare cases more (Lahore has many more Low than High events).
--- Shape #15 ---
VALIDATION
--- Shape #16 ---
StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5)
--- Shape #17 ---
We split the data into 5 chunks. We train on 4 chunks and test on the 5th, then rotate. The 'stratified' part makes sure each chunk has the same mix of High/Medium/Low cases — important because High is rare. We report the average accuracy across all 5 rotations.

--- Shape #6 ---
STATISTICAL FOUNDATION
--- Shape #7 ---
Crime counts in a time-window follow a Poisson process.
--- Shape #9 ---
P(= 1 crime next hour) = 1 - e^(-?)
--- Shape #10 ---
? (lambda) = expected number of crimes per hour, built up from history:
--- Shape #12 ---
? = base_lambda
    × dow_mult       (day-of-week)
    × month_mult     (seasonal)
    × hour_mult^2.2  (sharp diurnal)
--- Shape #14 ---
WHAT THIS MEANS
--- Shape #15 ---
Plain English: how likely is at least one crime in the next hour for this area? Higher ? means more crimes typically happen in this hour, day, month — so the probability rises. The 2.2 power on the hourly multiplier makes night-time risk visibly higher than daytime, which matches what citizens actually feel.

--- Shape #3 ---
Find the safest path between A and B — even if a few minutes longer
--- Shape #6 ---
THREE ROUTES SCORED
--- Shape #7 ---
Fastest
shortest time, ignores risk
Alternate
balanced compromise
Safest
minimum spatial risk along the line
--- Shape #10 ---
THE TRICK  ·  PERPENDICULAR VIA-POINTS
--- Shape #11 ---
Plain English: OSRM (the routing engine) doesn't naturally give us truly different alternates — they tend to look like the same path with small wiggles. Our trick: we pick a midpoint between A and B, then push that midpoint sideways (perpendicular) by a few hundred metres. We force OSRM to route THROUGH each shifted midpoint. Result: three genuinely different paths we can actually compare.
--- Shape #14 ---
THE SCORE
--- Shape #15 ---
overall_risk = 0.7 × avg_risk + 0.3 × max_risk
--- Shape #16 ---
If is_night (10pm – 4am):  score × 0.85
--- Shape #17 ---
70% on average risk rewards consistently safe corridors; 30% on the worst point on the route penalises any one nasty hotspot. After dark, every score drops 15% — pushing the 'safest' choice to differ from daytime.
