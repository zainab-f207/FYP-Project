ABSTRACT


In the modern fast changing digital age, the issue of citizen safety and immediate awareness of the incident has become topical among citizens and law enforcers. The classic crime mapping tools are largely based on proven crime records, which misses a huge gap of managing unconfirmed incident reports and offering proactive safety information. SafeVision is created to solve these problems as an intelligent AI-driven web-based safety and incident management platform that helps to bridge the gap between reported incidents and actionable safety intelligence. 
As opposed to traditional systems that only process the verified crimes, the SafeVision will process the First Information Reports (FIRs) but will always draw a line between reported cases and confirmed crimes. The platform allows citizens, administrators and law enforcement agencies to monitor, predict and act on safety issues in a single interface. 
The architecture of the platform includes three different levels of access SuperAdmin, Admin and User; it is written in FastAPI to provide a high-performance backend, React.js to have a responsive frontend interface, and MySQL to have secure and scalable data storage. SuperAdmins have complete control over the system including user control, registration of the administrator, validation of the FIR data, and access to classified records. Both SuperAdmins and Admins use a multi-layered AI-based prediction engine that integrates Poisson probability modeling with Random Forest classification to facilitate both the support of manual and multi-crime prediction by date, area, and incident type. The user interface offers interactive incident heatmaps which display route suggestions that prioritize safety along with real-time navigation and contact location sharing through messaging features and automated alerts for high-risk zones and expected incidents which users receive through email and browser notifications. 
The organization conducts testing and validation activities while developing the system to confirm its performance through ongoing accuracy testing and responsiveness testing and reliability testing. Early feedback demonstrates that SafeVision combines artificial intelligence with geospatial analytics and automated document processing and real-time alert systems to create a comprehensive public safety platform that provides predictive capabilities.

SafeVision functions as an AI-based web safety framework which employs advanced incident analysis to predict upcoming dangers while providing users with critical situation navigation and communication tools. The system integrates multiple technologies through its architectural design which combines artificial intelligence and geospatial analysis with automated notifications and optical character recognition to enable data verification and incident prediction and safety planning. SafeVision offers interactive displays which provide predictive insights to create safer metropolitan areas that use advanced technology.

The current system lacks both active alerts for potential threats and future risk assessment methods at different times for specific locations together with tools that enable officials to examine incident reports and identify key insights.

Introduction
The rapid increase of cities and online connections has created a greater demand for advanced public safety management systems which can assess current safety conditions and predict future safety situations. As incident frequency mounts and data origins stay separate, the imperative for unified platforms capable of deriving useful insights has become vital. The current section provides an evaluation of safety forecasting systems through their theoretical foundations and operational technologies which include existing systems and studies plus their existing limitations. Explanation of Important Constructs of the Application Domain
Grasping the theoretical bases of SafeVision necessitates scrutinizing several crucial constructions that collectively comprise the intellectual core of intelligent safety administration systems.
Public Safety Management
Public safety governance operates through technological tools which handle data and structured processes to conduct risk assessment and danger reduction activities. The previous systems operated through event response procedures which followed emergency occurrences while current systems employ data analysis and predictive modeling to prevent incidents from happening. SafeVision achieves this concept through its system which documents incidents and provides users with tools to predict threats through data analysis to enhance public safety.
Predictive Analytics in Safety
Predictive forecasting uses historical and current data to assess potential future threats. The system operates under the belief that past patterns will assist in predicting future events although researchers must manage challenges which arise from both data quality concerns and ethical research obligations. SafeVision uses two methods for its operation which include Poisson probability modeling to deliver precise percentage forecasts and Random Forest classification to identify complex patterns and categorize different risk levels.
Geospatial Intelligence
Geospatial awareness involves using location-specific data together with mapping technologies to investigate spatial patterns and their relationships across multiple areas. The security management system uses geospatial intelligence to identify dangerous areas while creating incident maps and assessing how threats vary across different locations. Decision-making depends on location data because incidents tend to gather in specific areas instead of spreading out evenly across space. SafeVision utilizes heatmaps to show dangerous areas while using radius-based security assessments and Haversine distance calculations to determine which incidents fall inside a user's notification range.
Alert and Advisory Systems
Notification systems deliver emergency alerts to users through immediate and practical safety information. The system connects analysis with operation because it transforms data understanding into useful direction. Good notice systems need to show the right information using clear language which requires users to keep reading until they reach full understanding of the situation. The system uses location-based filtering and cooldown protocols to prevent repeated alerts while sending out alerts through browser pushes and electronic mail and in-app warnings. The system includes direct routing hyperlinks which enable users to go directly to detailed content whenever they need it.
Statistical Probability Modeling
Statistical likelihood modeling uses mathematical equations to determine event probabilities based on existing data. Security systems use the Poisson distribution as a standard method to predict rare events throughout various time intervals. The technique provides a reliable approximation because real-world conditions present more complex challenges. SafeVision uses this model to calculate risk assessment percentages which include the risk of a crime occurring in a particular time and location. The method establishes standardization through probabilistic display of unpredictable events.
Machine Learning Classification
Machine learning classification uses historic examples to create data groups through pattern identification. Advanced statistical models enable the detection of intricate data connections which traditional statistical methods cannot identify. In SafeVision, a Random Forest Classifier uses previous incident data which includes location and offense type and time of day and season to divide risk levels into three categories: Elevated, Moderate, and Minimal. Random Forest implementation improves model accuracy by reducing overfitting, which makes it suitable for security datasets that follow structured formats.
Risk Scoring Mechanism
The risk appraisal system uses incident data to generate one numerical value which indicates the risk level present in that specific situation. The system offers more detailed safety assessments through its ability to assess danger levels for different safety classes. The risk assessment for SafeVision results from combining essential risk factors through their respective weightings which assign 35% to transgression frequency and 30% to immediacy and 15% to gravity and 10% to trajectory and 10% to temporal factor. The rating system operates to evaluate both the number of incidents and their timings and their evolving patterns.
Multi-Layered Risk Prediction Approach
SafeVision uses a three-level prediction system to maintain system reliability and continuous operational system functioning. The system combines different techniques into a defined sequence instead of using only one technique. The first level uses the Poisson model to create accurate probability predictions when there is sufficient available data. The system switches to the Random Forest model when the main method fails because of insufficient data. The system employs a basic label encoding system as a backup method to ensure users receive some form of risk assessment, which functions as a mandatory requirement. The tiered structure provides continuous system operation while it prevents failures through its ability to handle different types of data situations.

Layer 1 (Primary) - Poisson Probability Model: This layer executes first to compute the statistical likelihood of any specific incident (e.g., 22.4% probability of theft). The model achieves both computational efficiency and mathematical precision when historical data exists in sufficient amounts.

Layer 3 (Tertiary) - Legacy Label Encoder: This system backup tool ensures that every system operation will result in some type of system response because it prevents the occurrence of empty screens or system errors.

Modules of FYP
The SafeVision divides into six main components which each handle a specific range of functions. The following sections explain how the system developed each component.
Module 1:Validation and Permission Unit Purpose:
The Validation and Permission Unit manages user accounts through handling user registration and login and email validation and role-based access control and JWT token processing and session security.
Algorithmic Details
User Sign-up with Email Validation and Location Check:Receive sign-up submission; verify electronic mail and password strength; look for duplicate electronic mail; validate Lahore coordinates using Nominatim; obfuscate password with bcrypt; create electronic mail validation token; save user as unverified; dispatch validation electronic mail; record sign-up.
Sign-in Validation with Role-Based 2FA:The system processes sign-in requests by confirming user credentials while verifying their lockout status. The system resets failed login attempts after successful authentication. The system uses OTP authentication for admin and superadmin users while applying TOTP for all enabled user accounts. The system generates access tokens and refresh tokens for users while storing the refresh token. The system records every sign-in event to maintain security.


Module 2: Multi-Tiered AI Estimation Unit Purpose: 
The Multi-Tiered AI Estimation Unit uses three methods to estimate risk through its primary method of Poisson Probability and its secondary method of Random Forest and its Legacy method.
Algorithmic Specifics:
Poisson Likelihood Computation:Retrieve historical occurrences; determine lambda from occurrences per interval; return FAIL if lambda is zero; compute P(X>=1)=1-e^(-lambda); convert to percentage; round to single decimal spot; return hazard proportion.
Random Forest Training:Collect authorized incident records; prepare and encode categorical fields; construct time/season features; part data; train Random Forest with balanced sets; assess framework; store framework, encoders, and scaler.
Random Forest Estimation:Fetch framework artifacts; derive features from zone, incident type, time, and day; encode and scale features; estimate likelihoods and class; relate class to Low/Medium/High; return hazard tier and certainty.
Legacy Backup Estimation:The system allocates scores through pre-set zone hazard tiers and incident seriousness tiers; it merges the scores which lead to High/Medium/Low; and it provides a low-certainty backup outcome.
Unified Estimation Access Point:The first step of the process involves testing Poisson; if it produces a value the system will link to the hazard tier while registering the outcome; if Poisson fails the system will proceed to test Random Forest; if Random Forest also fails the system will use Legacy backup; the system will always generate an estimation result.
Module 3: Hazard Rating and Safety Rating UnitÂ Purpose: 
This unit computes unified hazard and safety ratings using weighted factors: quantity, newness, seriousness, progression, and timing.
Algorithmic Specifics:
Unified Hazard Rating Computation: Fetch incidents by zone or radius; gauge quantity, newness, seriousness, progression, and timing inputs; apply weights 35/30/15/10/10; compute hazard rating and safety rating; apply decay and safe-by-default stipulations; return factor breakdown.
Module 4:OCR and FIR Data UnitÂ Purpose:
This unit extracts structured details from FIR images using EasyOCR, PaddleOCR, Tesseract, and OpenCV preprocessing.
Algorithm Specifics:
Image Preprocessing Pipeline: Load FIR visual; convert to monochrome; apply CLAHE, softening, and adaptive demarcation; eliminate horizontal and vertical traces; magnify visual for clarity; return the preprocessed visual.
Multi-Engine OCR with Field Extraction: Operate EasyOCR, PaddleOCR, and Tesseract; select the superior certainty outcome; pull out the incident zone using OCR patterns and strip scanning; standardize zone identifications; pull out sections, date, and time; locate the zone; return structured FIR details.
Module 5: Warning and Delivery UnitÂ Purpose: 
This unit oversees incidents and conveys alerts via browser push and electronic mail with cooldown logic.
Algorithmic Specifics:
Haversine Distance Computation: Convert coordinates to radians; compute latitude and longitude variations; apply the Haversine formula; return the separation in kilometers.
Background Alert Oversight: Execute every 5 minutes; query newly approved incidents; locate confirmed users inside alert perimeter; compute separations to home and work spots; process solely affected users; mark incidents as alerted following processing.
Alert Processing with Cooldown: Compose a cooldown key per user and zone; bypass if the 60-minute cooldown is active; establish hazard tier and hue; generate a deep link; insert alert record; dispatch push and/or electronic mail; revise cooldown cache; log dispatch.
Browser Push Notification Setup:If VAPID keys don't exist you need to create them first before getting browser approval while you need to use the service operator to send subscription details to the backend system after that you will need to keep the subscription active until the time arrives for push notifications.
Module 6:Geospatial Rendering Unit Purpose: 
This unit uses Leaflet and OpenStreetMap to show maps and heatmaps along with markers and route safety suggestions.
Algorithmic Specifics:
Heatmap Fabrication:The system queries incidents based on the selected geographic area and its filtering options while assigning intensity levels according to the severity of incidents which results in creating heatmap points collection that the system uses to render Leaflet heatmap display.
Safety Route Computation:The base route is obtained from OSRM after which the route gets divided into sections which require hazard and safety calculations based on nearby incidents for each section. The system selects a safer route whenever the average safety rating falls below the established threshold. The route description should include both its geometric layout and safety assessment details.
